The Arithmetic Shadow of Galois Theory

Explore how the powers of the fundamental unit $\alpha = 1 + \sqrt{2}$ generate Pell numbers through algebraic conjugation in the quadratic field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$.

Interactive Field Explorer

1. Expanding Powers of $\alpha^n$

Notice how the coefficient of √2 matches the Pell Number ($P_n$).

(1 + √2)³ = 7 + 5√2
When $n = 3$, $x_3 = 7$ and $y_3 = 5$. Thus, the 3rd Pell Number is 5.

2. The Galois Automorphism ($\sigma$)

The non-trivial element of $\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})/\mathbb{Q})$ maps √2 ↦ -√2.

σ(7 + 5√2) = 7 - 5√2

3. The Field Norm: $N(\alpha^n) = x_n^2 - 2y_n^2$

Multiplying the field element by its Galois conjugate eliminates the irrational part entirely.

(7 + 5√2)(7 - 5√2) = 7² - 2(5²) = 49 - 50 = -1
This satisfies Pell's Equation: $7^2 - 2(5)^2 = (-1)^3 = -1$.
